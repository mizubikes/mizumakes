PETG is the filament you reach for when a PLA part would sag, snap or soften. Brackets, enclosures, jigs and clips are the usual jobs. If you're still deciding between the two, my [PLA vs PETG comparison](/posts/pla-vs-petg/) covers the basics. This post is about which PETG brand to buy once you've decided.

A caveat first. I didn't run a tensile rig for this. Everything below comes from manufacturer specs and published roundups, and those roundups often disagree with each other. Treat the rankings as a starting point and the spec sheets as the firmer ground.

## What matters for a functional part
For structural prints, the property that matters most is layer adhesion, because FDM parts fail between layers first. After that comes diameter consistency, which affects flow and dimensional accuracy over a long print. Price comes last, since for a hidden bracket you can usually put the savings toward more spools.

The tolerance numbers need some skepticism. One roundup lists Overture at <cite index="2-10">±0.02 mm</cite>, while another lists the same brand at <cite index="3-8">±0.03 mm</cite>. Check the manufacturer's page for the exact spool you're buying.

None of this works on a wet spool. PETG typically prints at roughly <cite index="23-1">230-250°C nozzle and 70-90°C bed</cite>. One roundup advises <cite index="5-19">drying the spool first and printing a little hotter</cite> for load-bearing parts. Bambu's own drying spec for its PETG is <cite index="14-3">65°C for 8 hours</cite>.

## Prusament PETG: the consistency pick
Prusa makes its own filament and <cite index="35-6">guarantees ±0.02 mm precision</cite> on standard PETG. Each spool also comes with <cite index="33-8">measured diameter, weight and ovality data</cite>. For parts that need to fit, or a batch you want to repeat months later, that traceability is the real selling point. One roundup says it's <cite index="1-3">among the highest in Z-axis tensile strength in community testing</cite>.

The price is the catch. Estimates vary: one 2026 snapshot put baseline Prusament PETG at <cite index="28-2">$25.49 per kilo</cite>, while other roundups run into the mid-$30s. One of them calls it <cite index="17-10">about twice the price of eSUN or Overture</cite>. Check the current price before you assume. [Prusament PETG on Amazon](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Prusament+PETG+1.75mm+1kg&tag=mizubikes-web-20) is worth a look, though Prusa's own store is the usual source.

## Polymaker: strong and color-consistent
Polymaker comes up repeatedly as the strength and finish pick. One roundup credits Polymaker's newer high-speed PETG with <cite index="2-16">near 100% layer bonding in third-party testing</cite>. Its drawbacks there were <cite index="12-16">weaker heat resistance than older formulas and a glossy finish that shows surface flaws</cite>. Heat resistance matters if the part sits in a warm car or next to a motor, so weigh it.

Polymaker also <cite index="11-1">uses Pantone matching, so colors stay consistent between spools</cite>. That helps if you reorder to match an existing assembly. A step up is PolyMax PETG, which one roundup describes as having <cite index="19-3">roughly double the impact strength of standard PETG</cite> at around $30 per kilo. [Polymaker PETG](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Polymaker+PETG+filament+1.75mm&tag=mizubikes-web-20) is the everyday option.

## The budget tier: Overture, eSUN, Sunlu and Elegoo
For most brackets and organizers, the budget brands are fine. Overture gets the most consistent praise for being easy. One roundup calls it <cite index="5-4">one of the most forgiving PETGs to dial in, with less stringing than most budget options</cite>. Pricing is aggressive. A June 2026 price check found Overture's plain PETG sold as <cite index="22-6">a 2 kg twin pack at $26.99</cite>. Search for [Overture PETG](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Overture+PETG+filament+1.75mm&tag=mizubikes-web-20) and compare the multipacks.

eSUN gets the strength nod in a couple of roundups. One described it as <cite index="6-10">slightly better on layer adhesion than Overture, but a bit fussier on the first layer</cite>. That's a reasonable trade for a part that will be loaded. See [eSUN PETG](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=eSUN+PETG+filament+1.75mm&tag=mizubikes-web-20).

Sunlu is usually the cheapest. The common caveat is that it <cite index="5-14">strings a bit more than premium lines but prints reliably once you tune retraction</cite>. If you're printing several kilos of shop fixtures, that tuning session pays for itself. Here's [Sunlu PETG](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=SUNLU+PETG+filament+1.75mm&tag=mizubikes-web-20).

Elegoo is the one to watch on price. Its own store listed a PETG HF line at <cite index="20-5">$13.99 in 12 colors</cite>. I couldn't find independent strength data for it, so I'd treat it as a cheap, unproven option. [Elegoo PETG](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Elegoo+PETG+filament+1.75mm&tag=mizubikes-web-20) is there if you want to try a spool.

## Bambu Lab: the PETG HF situation
If you own a Bambu printer, the lineup has changed. Bambu's US store says <cite index="39-2">PETG HF is discontinued and won't be restocked once sold out</cite>. Several reports say a reformulated PETG Basic replaced it. On Bambu's own data sheets, as relayed by one comparison, Basic is stronger: <cite index="14-2">75 vs 64 MPa bending strength and 56 vs 48 MPa layer strength</cite>.

HF's advantage was speed. The lower viscosity that lets HF print fast also <cite index="11-3">slightly reduces toughness compared to standard PETG</cite>. For functional parts that's the wrong trade unless you're printing in volume. A reformulated Basic that's slower and stronger suits most of the parts I'd want to make.

Price is muddy. One review cites a <cite index="45-11">$20 refill price for PETG Basic</cite>. A third-party retailer listed spooled versions at <cite index="27-4">$33.99 to $35.99</cite>. Refills versus spools, and direct versus resellers, probably explain most of the gap. [Bambu Lab PETG Basic](https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Bambu+Lab+PETG+Basic&tag=mizubikes-web-20) is the one to search for. Don't assume it works for everyone: one forum user reported stringing problems and better results from a different brand. If you're deciding on a printer to go with it, I compared two of them in [the P1S vs P2S breakdown](/posts/bambu-p1s-sale-vs-p2s/).

## How I'd choose
For general shop parts like holders, clips and covers, buy a budget brand. Overture is the easiest start, and eSUN is worth trying if layer strength matters. Buy in multipacks when you can.

For parts with tight fits, repeat production, or anything where you'd want to know exactly what went into the print, Prusament's guaranteed tolerance and per-spool data justify the extra cost. If you want strength and don't mind the gloss, Polymaker is a good middle option.

On a Bambu machine, PETG Basic gives you automatic profiles and AMS convenience. A good third-party spool works fine too.

One limit to remember: PETG isn't the answer for everything. If the part lives outdoors in strong sun or needs more heat resistance, look at ASA. I wrote about [when to stop using PETG outdoors](/posts/what-is-asa-filament/).

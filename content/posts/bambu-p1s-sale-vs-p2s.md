## My call

At $170, this is genuinely close, and closer than it was a week ago â€” the P2S combo was $699 until recently, and at that price I would have said buy it without much hesitation.

The case for spending it is the extruder. Grinding detection and auto flow calibration are the two features that actually reduce failed prints, and failed prints are the real cost of this hobby, not the printer. If you leave prints running unattended, $170 to catch a clog before it becomes a bird's nest is cheap.

The case against is that $170 is most of a spare AMS, or six or seven rolls of decent filament.

But I want to be straight about the counterargument, because it's strong, and my own machine is the evidence for it. Twelve hundred hours in, my P1S has needed nozzles and build plate maintenance and nothing else. No motion system drift, no board failures, no mystery quality regression. That's a three-year-old design with an enormous print-farm install base behind it, and it shows. At $579 with a current-generation AMS 2 Pro, nothing else in the market is close on price-per-capability.

I'll be honest about what my hours do and don't prove, though. They're strong evidence the P1S won't fall apart on you. They're not evidence the P2S extruder isn't better at catching clogs, because I've never run one â€” the failures I've had were filament problems I caught manually, and a machine that catches them for me would have saved me time I didn't count.

So:

**Get the P2S combo at $749 if** you print a variety of filaments, you buy third-party spools, you're going to leave prints running unattended, or this is your first printer and you'd rather the machine handle calibration than learn to do it yourself.

**Get the P1S AMS 2 Pro combo at $579 if** you're price-sensitive, you're comfortable dialing in a flow ratio once per filament, or you're adding a second machine to a setup where you already know your profiles. Manual flow calibration is ten minutes of work, not a skill barrier.

**Get the P1S solo at $399 if** you genuinely only print single-color functional parts. That's most of what comes off my machines, and $399 is the cheapest credible path to an enclosed CoreXY. Just be honest with yourself about whether "I'll never need multicolor" survives contact with your first year of printing â€” because upgrading later costs $299 instead of $180.

**Get neither if** you don't need an enclosure. The A1 at $299 or the A1 Mini at $219 handles PLA and PETG for a lot less, and an enclosure you don't need is a cooling problem, not a feature.
